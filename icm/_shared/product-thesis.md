# Product Thesis

## Category
Grant Agent is not an AI grant writer. It is a **Funding Operating System / invisible grants department** for nonprofits.

## Customer job
When a nonprofit needs to fund a program, the team needs to know which opportunities are worth pursuing and turn organizational context into complete, compliant work without re-explaining the organization, hunting for files, living in databases, or retyping the same answers.

## Current barrier
The work is fragmented across grant databases, browsers, portals, email, Drive/Dropbox, spreadsheets, old applications, budgets, staff memory, calendars, and funder websites. Writing is only one step.

## Distinctive value
Grant Agent continuously compounds organizational context, uses deterministic/source-cited discovery before model judgment, coordinates specialist agents under one orchestrator, can operate authorized tools and grant portals, works multiplayer with the human team, and supports a local/private execution mode.

## Positioning statement
For nonprofit teams without enough grant capacity, Grant Agent is the funding operating system that already knows the organization and turns funding needs into source-cited opportunities and prepared work. Unlike grant databases or AI writing tools, it can coordinate the full lifecycle, work with the whole team, operate connected tools under explicit permissions, and preserve portable institutional memory in ICM.

## Product promise
**Tell it what needs funding. It handles the grant work and brings people in for judgment.**

## What we refuse
- chat-with-your-files dressed up as a grants department
- generic AI prose without source grounding
- opaque fit scores with no evidence
- autonomous submission without an explicit permission policy
- dashboards as the primary experience
- per-user knowledge silos
- hidden vendor lock-in
- extracting paid databases in violation of their terms

## Measured outcomes
Do not claim savings until measured. Track:
- time from funding need -> qualified shortlist
- researcher hours per qualified opportunity
- writer/reviewer hours per application
- percent of candidate opportunities rejected before writing
- deadline misses
- application completeness/error rate
- submission throughput
- award rate and award dollars, with attribution caveats
- report/renewal on-time rate
- team questions answered without blocking on one person
